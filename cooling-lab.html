When one temperature is not enough

  • Properties change with temperature, or phase change.
  • Heat enters one place, or one face is insulated.

Derivation notes

Assumptions

  1. The part is a long square bar (300 mm long, side w) cooling through its four long faces, so A = 4·w·L and V = w²·L. The ends are insulated.
  2. One uniform temperature describes the whole part (lumped capacitance). This is only trusted when Bi < 0.1.
  3. Density, specific heat and conductivity are constant (values in the footer).
  4. The surroundings stay at a constant T∞ and the heat transfer coefficient h is constant in the analytical model. The simulation adds radiation to h, and treats the furnace walls as the surroundings.
  5. No internal heat generation, no melting or other phase change (hot starts and the furnace are capped below the melting point).
  6. Water quench uses one constant h, with no boiling curve. The torch gives up to 350 W into one end and delivers less as that end nears the flame temperature.

Derivation, step by step

Step 1. Energy balance. The energy stored in the part changes only because heat leaves through its surface by convection.

m·c·dT/dt = −h·A·(T − T∞) , m = ρ·V

Step 2. Use the excess temperature. Put θ = T − T∞. Because T∞ is constant, dθ/dt = dT/dt, so

dθ/dt = −(h·A / ρ·c·V)·θ = −θ/τ , τ = ρ·c·V/(h·A)

Step 3. Separate the variables and integrate from θ₀ at t = 0 to θ at time t.

∫ dθ/θ = −∫ dt/τ → ln(θ/θ₀) = −t/τ

Step 4. Solution. A plot of ln θ against t is a straight line of slope −1/τ.

θ = θ₀·e^(−t/τ) , T(t) = T∞ + (T₀ − T∞)·e^(−t/τ)

Step 5. The three quantities. Temperature measures intensity, stored energy measures heat content, and q measures how fast energy leaves.

T(t) = T∞ + θ₀·e^(−t/τ)
E(t) = m·c·θ = m·c·θ₀·e^(−t/τ)
q(t) = h·A·θ = (m·c·θ₀/τ)·e^(−t/τ) = −dE/dt

Step 6. Energy check. All the stored energy must leave eventually.

∫₀^∞ q dt = m·c·θ₀

Step 7. When is one temperature allowed? Compare the resistance to conduction inside the part with the resistance to convection at its surface. For a long square bar Lc = V/A = w/4.

Bi = h·L꜀/k , Bi < 0.1 → lumped model valid

Step 8. Spatial form used for the checks and for the torch case, where one temperature is not enough.

ρc ∂T/∂t = k ∂²T/∂x² − (hP/A)(T − T∞)
Steel: ρ 7850, c 490, k 45. Copper: ρ 8960, c 385, k 401. Aluminium: ρ 2700, c 900, k 237. Constant properties, insulated bar ends, constant quench h (no boiling curve). Furnace: convection plus radiation to the walls. The torch delivers less as the end nears the 1900 °C flame. Melting points: steel 1450, copper 1085, aluminium 660 °C; the model ignores melting, so hot starts and the furnace are capped below it. Plume and bubbles are emitted in proportion to the real convective heat flow and drawn in slow motion (0.4×). Solver speed is capped where the 2D section would go unstable. The 3D cut face is a 2D explicit conduction solution of the square section.
